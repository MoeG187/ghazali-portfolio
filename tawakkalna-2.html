001Overview

The digital front door to a country.

Tawakkalna was born during COVID-19 as a national health-tracking app, but quickly became something far bigger: the digital front door to nearly every government service in Saudi Arabia.

By 2022, it was used daily by over 26 million people for everything from permits to school records — but the experience hadn't kept up. The interface was inherited from the emergency build: structured, bureaucratic, and built around government org charts rather than the people using it.

This redesign was the first major opportunity to rethink the app from the ground up — not as a service catalogue, but as a citizen-centered platform for the next decade of Saudi digital life.

002The Challenge

Built around entities, not citizens.

The original Tawakkalna was structured around how government entities organized their services internally — not around how citizens actually think or navigate.

Users couldn't find what they needed. The app felt like a bureaucratic index rather than a helpful digital service: deep menus, unfamiliar terminology, and a hierarchy that mirrored ministries instead of mental models.

003The Solution

Reframe the app around citizen intent.

I led benchmarking of global super-apps to identify structural patterns, then ran rapid ideation sessions, built prototypes, and validated through three rounds of usability testing with real users — all before a line of production code was written.

Two pivotal decisions emerged from research:

  • National Address was the most-accessed service. It was elevated to an anchor position on the home screen.
  • Users mentally categorised their passport as a document, not a service. It was moved out of Services into a dedicated Docs section.
