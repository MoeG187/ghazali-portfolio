001Overview

The mandate, and the risk.

Tawakkalna's role expanded faster than its design system could keep up. After the 2.0 redesign, the mandate was clear: every major government entity in the Kingdom would plug their services into the app.

Within two years, that meant integrating 2,000+ services from 40+ entities — each with different content standards, naming conventions, and design maturity.

The risk wasn't that the app would fail. It was that it would slowly degrade — drowning the citizen experience under entity-driven complexity. This case study is about how UX governance, not just UX design, was used to protect a national platform at scale.

002The Challenge

Scale degrades clarity — unless something protects it.

Services arrived in uncontrolled patches from 40+ government entities, each with different structures, naming conventions, and content quality.

The core risk was that scale would destroy the clarity the redesign had created. No matter how good the framework, without enforced standards the citizen experience would collapse under its own weight.

003The Solution

A three-layer IA, plus a governance layer.

I designed a 3-layer information architecture so the app could scale without losing the citizen as its centre of gravity:

  • Search-first navigation as the primary mode. Validated through competitor benchmarking and early-build usage data.
  • Smart categorisation as the secondary backbone. Built around citizen intent rather than entity taxonomy.
  • Personalisation as the tertiary layer. Surfacing what each citizen actually uses, when they use it.

In parallel, I created formal service-onboarding guidelines and ran structured workshops with government entity teams to set UX standards before their services went live. The governance layer was as important as the design layer.
